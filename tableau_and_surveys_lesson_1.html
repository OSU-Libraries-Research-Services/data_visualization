Lesson 1. Prepare Survey Data

👷🚧 This page is under construction 🚧👷

This lesson introduces a practical workflow for preparing survey data for analysis and visualization in Tableau. Using the 2024 Citywide Mobility Survey from New York City’s OpenData, it explores how to assess survey structure and documentation, identify dimensions and measures, and clean and reshape data to support effective analysis.

Learning Objectives

  1. Evaluate survey datasets and metadata to understand survey structure, questions, and response formats.
  2. Create and document a data dictionary that describes survey variables, response options, and coding schemes.
  3. Apply common data cleaning and preparation techniques required for survey analysis and visualization.
  4. Pivot and reshape survey data to support analysis and visualization in Tableau.

This tutorial supports The Ohio State University Libraries Research Commons Tableau and Surveys workshop series. Building on concepts introduced in the Tableau for Research workshops, it introduces INTERMEDIATE Tableau users to techniques for working with, analyzing, and communicating survey data in Tableau.

LESSON 1

Step 1. Clarify research question

The first step for any survey analysis and visualization project is to clarify your research question or analysis goal. Because the Citywide Mobility Survey is so large and complex, let’s focus on a single mode of transportation: Bicycles.

The Citywide Mobility Survey 2024 includes questions about bicycle ownership, bicycle use, e-bikes, and barriers to cycling. What research questions might a data analyst working for The New York City Department of Transportation consider to help inform bicycle infrastructure needs, such as the addition of bike lanes, bicycle parking, and support for e-bikes?

Research Questions
  • Who is most likely to ride a bicycle in New York City?
  • How does bicycle use vary by age, income, employment status, or education level?
  • What are the most common reasons for riding a bicycle?
  • What factors prevent residents from biking more often?
  • How has bicycle use increased or decreased for different groups of residents?
  • Are e-bike owners different from traditional bicycle owners?

Step 2. Evaluate dataset

The next step is to critically evaluate the dataset, its context, and structure. Look for a description of the dataset and consider:

  • Who created the dataset and why?
  • What procedures were used to collect the data?
  • What information is included, and what is missing?
  • Are there any potential sources of bias in the data that have not been disclosed?
  • When was the data collected or last updated?
  • Does the data still reflect current trends?

Consider whether the dataset contains the information needed to answer your question(s) and whether the data collection methods are appropriate for your field or area of study. If the dataset does not adequately support your objectives, you may need to identify a different data source or combine it with additional datasets.

Locate the 2024 Citywide Mobility Survey in the NYC OpenData portal. Review the dataset description and documentation, then use the questions in Step 2 to critically evaluate the dataset.

Step 3. Evaluate metadata

Review the survey’s metadata and underlying data structure. Metadata, or data about data, documents variable definitions, units of measurement, data types, survey question types, and relationships among files. Understanding this information upfront is essential for efficient analysis, accurate interpretations and effective visualization.

Review the survey questions to identify the dimensions (categorical variables) and measures (quantitative variables) relevant to your research question. Pay close attention to how each variable is defined and how survey responses are coded. For example, are numeric values used to represent text responses?

Within the survey dataset itself, consider the following questions:

  • What field serves as the unique identifier for each survey response?
  • Are datatypes consistent within a column or series?
  • How are Null or missing values handled?
  • Are survey weights included, and if so, how should they be applied?
  • If the survey data is stored across multiple tables or files, how are the relationships between them defined?

Finally, examine the structure of the dataset. Is the dataset organized in wide format (many columns and fewer rows) or a tall format (fewer columns and more rows)? Will the data need to be pivoted or reshaped before it can be analyzed in Tableau?

In wide data, each respondent is represented by a single row, with survey responses stored across multiple columns.

Step 5. Prepare data

Taking time to identify necessary data preparation steps before beginning analysis and visualization can save considerable time and frustration later. Before reshaping any data, determine whether to clean it in Tableau, or before you connect the data to Tableau. If only a few changes are needed, Tableau’s data grouping and calculated fields features are often sufficient. If extensive cleaning or transformation is required, however, it is generally more efficient to prepare the data before importing it into Tableau. This approach can improve Tableau workbook performance, simplify your workflow, and help keep your Tableau workbook organized.

Some common data preparation tasks include:

  • Standardizing names, locations, and formats (“OH” = “Ohio”)
  • Removing unnecessary punctuation, including leading and trailing spaces
  • Eliminating duplicate rows, when appropriate
  • Converting text to consistent case (e.g. all lowercase)
  • Replacing null values with zeros or other meaningful defaults
  • Standardizing dates and time formats
  • Splitting or unmerging columns
  • Verifying and correcting data types
  • Pivoting or reshaping data from wide to tall to support analysis and visualization

There are many tools available for data preparation. Simple tasks can often be completed in Excel or Google Sheets, while more complex workflows may be better handled with Tableau Prep, R, or Python. The best choice depends on the size and complexity of your dataset, the transformations you need to perform, and the tools you’re most comfortable using.

Tableau Prep

Tableau Prep is a visual, drag-and-drop tool used to clean, combine, and transform data from multiple sources. Tableau Prep is particularly useful for preparing survey data, as the tool documents each step taken to prepare data for analysis.

To prepare the 2024 Citywide Mobility Survey data to answer the research questions introduced in Exercise 1 using Tableau Prep, start by connecting the data in Tableau Prep Builder.

Step 5.1. Connect to data

On Tableau Prep’s Start Page

  1. In the Connections pane, click the + to create a new connection.
  2. Connect to the HOUSEHOLD, PERSON, AND TRIP files downloaded in Exercise 3.
  3. As each file is added, right-click the input and rename it as follows:
  • Citywide_Mobility_Survey_-_Household_2024.csv → HOUSEHOLD
  • Citywide_Mobility_Survey_-_Person_2024.csv → PERSON
  • Citywide_Mobility_Survey_-_Trip_2024.csv → TRIP
  1. The three input tables will appear in the Flow pane. Selecting an input table displays its contents in the Input pane directly below the Flow pane. Use the ^ on the right side of the Flow pane to expand or collapse the pane as needed.

Step 5.2. Isolate variables

For each input table, retain only the dimensions and measures identified in Step 4 Removing unnecessary fields reduces the size of the dataset, simplifies preparation, and improves Tableau workbook performance.

  • Shift + click to select a range of consecutive rows.
  • Ctrl + click (Windows) or Cmd + click (Mac) to select multiple individual, non-adjacent rows

Step 5.3. Reshape data

To optimize survey data for analysis in Tableau, create demographic and weights tables in a wide format and a survey response table in a long (pivoted) format. The demographic and weights tables should contain one row per Respondent ID, while the survey response table should contain multiple rows per Respondent ID, with one row for each survey question and response. These tables can then be related using the Respondent ID field.

Keeping demographic and weight data in a wide format preserves a single record for each respondent, making it easier to filter, group, segment, and properly weight variables without introducing duplicate records or affecting aggregations. Survey question responses should be pivoted into a long format to support flexible analysis and visualization across many questions. This structure provides an effective balance of performance, usability, and analytical flexibility in Tableau.

Demographics

To create the DEMOGRAPHICS table:

  1. Click the + sign to the right of the HOUSEHOLD table and select Clean Step. This creates a branch from the HOUSEHOLD table where you can filter, rename, and transform fields without modifying the original table.
  2. In the Input pane, switch from Profile view to Field List view by clicking the Show Field List icon to the left of the Search Box.
  3. Retain only the fields:
    • hh_id (this is the [Respondent ID])
    • income_broad
  4. In the Flow Pane, right-click the Clean Step and rename to H_DEMOGRAPHICS.
  5. Repeat Steps 2-4 for the PERSON table. Retain only the following fields:
    • hh_id
    • person_id
    • age
    • can_drive
    • education
    • employment
    • ethnicity
    • gender
    • industry
    • race
    • relationship
    • student
    Rename the resulting step P_DEMOGRAPHICS.
  6. Drag P_DEMOGRAPHICS onto H_DEMOGRAPHICS and drop it on the Join icon when it appears. The Join Profile will open in the Input pane. Verify the Join Clause is H_DEMOGRAPHICS hh_id = P_DEMOGRAPHICS hh_id and the Join Type is Inner. Switch from Profile view to Field List view and remove the duplicate hh_id (hh_id_-1). Rename the resulting step DEMOGRAPHICS

Weights

To create the WEIGHTS tables:

  1. Create a new branch from the HOUSEHOLD table by clicking the + sign to the right of HOUSEHOLD and selecting Clean Step. IMPORTANT: Be sure to select the + sign attached directly to the HOUSEHOLD household table. Selecting the + icon on an existing black flow line will add a new step to the H_DEMOGRAPHICS branch.
  2. Rename the step H_WEIGHTS
  3. Retain the following fields:
    • hh_id
    • hh_weight_zonal
    • hh_weight_citywide
  4. Create a new branch from the PERSON table, rename the step P_WEIGHTS and retain the following fields:
    • hh_id
    • person_id
    • person_weight_zonal
    • person_weight_citywide
  5. Create a new branch from the TRIP table, rename the step T_WEIGHTS and retain the following fields:
    • hh_id
    • person_id
    • trip_id
    • trip_weight_zonal
    • trip_weight_citywide

Survey Responses

To create the SURVEY_RESPONSES table:

  1. Create a new branch from the HOUSEHOLD table by clicking the + sign to the right of HOUSEHOLD and selecting Clean Step. Rename the step S_HOUSEHOLD and retain all fields except:
    • hh_weight_zonal
    • hh_weight_citywide
  2. Repeat Step 1 for the PERSON table, renaming the step S_PERSON and retaining all fields except:
    • person_weight_zonal
    • person_weight_citywide
  3. Repeat Step 1 for the TRIP table, renaming the step S_TRIP and retaining all fields except:
    • trip_weight_zonal
    • trip_weight_citywide
  4. Union the three survey response tables by dragging S_PERSON onto S_HOUSEHOLD and dropping it on the Union icon when it appears. Then drag S_TRIP onto the resulting union and drop it on the Add to Union icon. Remove the Table Names field. Rename the resulting step SURVEY_RESPONSES.
  5. Pivot the survey response table. Click the + sign to the right of the SURVEY_RESPONSES step and select Pivot. Drop all fields into the Pivot Fields pane except:
    • hh_id
    • person_id
  6. Rename:
    • Pivot1 Values → column_abbreviation
    • Pivot1 Names → numeric_value
  7. Filter the numeric_value field to exclude Null values.
  8. Rename the pivot step SURVEY_RESPONSES_PIVOT. The SURVEY_RESPONSES_PIVOT table now contains one row per respondent per survey question

Step 5.4. Output files

  1. Click the + sign to the right of the DEMOGRAPHICS step and select Output Step. Rename the output step D_OUTPUT and select Comma Separated Values (.csv) as the Output Type. Click Browse to select a location to save the file, name the file demographics.csv, and click Accept. Then click Run Flow. This will create the demographics.csv file in the location you specified.
  2. Repeat Step 1 for each WEIGHTS step.
    • For H_WEIGHTS, name the output file household_weights.csv
    • For P_WEIGHTS, name the output file person_weights.csv
    • For T_WEIGHTS, name the output file trip_weights.csv
    Click Run Flow to create the output.
  3. Repeat Step 1 for the SURVEY_RESPONSES_PIVOT step and name the output file survey_responses.csv. Click Run Flow to create the output.

The following .csv files are now ready for analysis in Tableau:

  • demographics.csv
  • household_weights.csv
  • person_weights.csv
  • trip_weights.csv
  • survey_responses.csv

Step 6. Modify|Create data dictionary

The Citywide_Mobility_Survey-_Datasets-Collections_2024.xlsx file provides an overview of HOUSEHOLD, PERSON, DAY, TRIP, and VEHICLE datasets. It documents the purpose of each table, defines the variables contained within them, and explains the coding used for the survey responses. A relationship diagram illustrating how each table is connected using the household, person, and day identifiers is also included, as well as a tab disclosing the dataset’s revision history.

After pivoting the survey response fields, we can use the column abbreviation with the numeric value variables to relate our survey responses to the original survey questions and their corresponding text values. To support this process, however, the Column Information worksheet in the Citywide_Mobility_Survey-_Datasets-Collections_2024.xlsx file must be restrctured into a more analysis-friendly data dictionary.

To update the Column Information worksheet:

  • Remove the Citywide Mobility Survey Tables title row so that the column headers in row 2 become the first row of the worksheet.
  • Restructure the worksheet so that it contains one row for each survey question-response combination.
  • Split the existing Expected/Allowed Values field into two separate columns:
    • numeric_value: the numeric code associated with the response.
    • column_abbreviation: the correspnding survey response variable abbreviation (column name).
  • Using the NYC_Citywide_Mobility_Survey_2024_Questionnaire, lookup each survey variable abbreviation and add:
    • question_number: the survey question number.
    • question_text: the full text of the survey question.
  • For matrix questions, add a row_item column containing the full text of the matrix row associated with each response.

For example, the matrix question “What are the reasons you do not ride a bicycle more often?” has multiple row items, including “I do not own a bicycle” and “I do not feel safe riding a bicycle.” Each item should be recorded in the row_item column alongside its corresponding survey response.

The data_dictionary_bikes.csv file contains a preprocessed version of the data dictionary that includes the variables needed to answer the research questions introduced in Exercise 1. Each record represents a single survey question-response combination and includes the question number, question text, row item (when applicable), column abbreviation, and numeric value.

Step 7. Relate datasets

The final step in preparing the survey data for analysis in Tableau is to relate the demographic, weights, survey response, and data dictionary tables.

  1. Open Tableau Desktop and connect to the five .csv files created in Step 5 and the data_dictionary_bikes.csv file.
    • survey_responses.csv
    • data_dictionary.csv
    • demographics.csv
    • household_weights.csv
    • person_weights.csv
    • trip_weights.csv
  2. Drag and drop the survey_response.csv table to the canvas. Then create a relationship by dragging the data_dictionary_bikes.csv table to the canvas. When you see the blue “noodle” between the two tables, drop data_dictionary_bikes.csv. In the relationship settings, set the relationship as:
    • Column Abbreviation = Column Abbreviation
    • Numeric Value = Numeric Value
  3. Repeat step 2 to relate demographics.csv to the survey_responses.csv table and set the relationship as:
    • Hh Id = Hh Id
    • Person Id = Person Id
    • Note: You may need to select Edit Calculation to change the data type for Person Id from string to integer.
  4. Repeat step 2 to relate household_weights.csv to the survey_responses.csv table and set the relationship as:
    • Hh Id = Hh Id
  5. Repeat step 2 to relate person_weights.csv to the survey_responses.csv table and sset the relationship as:
    • Hh Id = Hh Id
    • Person Id = Person Id
  6. Repeat step 2 to relate trip_weights.csv to the survey_responses.csv table and set the relationship as:
    • Hh Id = Hh Id
    • Person Id = Person Id

With the survey data prepared and related in Tableau, the foundation is now in place for analysis and visualization. The next lesson explores various approaches to effectively and efficiently visualize demographic data.
